# Reading and revision

A reader uses questions to follow a book as they read and to strengthen their memory of its ideas over time.

## Language

**Quick recall check**  
A brief question at a meaningful pause that asks the reader to retrieve an essential detail or idea from the passage they just read. A few words recalled silently can be enough.

**Ephemeral question**  
A brief question encountered within a section to help the reader sustain attention and follow what they are reading. Depending on the reader’s choice during book preparation, it can also explicitly rehearse material for one or more later practice questions.

**Practice question**  
A question intended for repeated retrieval in Practice. The reader can encounter a book’s practice question first at its section checkpoint and review the same question later as a card.

**Promotion**  
A reader’s choice to keep an ephemeral question for repeated review in Practice. The reader can keep the original wording, edit it themselves, or steer a rewrite by explaining what they want to remember.

**Higher-order comprehension check**  
In the graduated pathway, a practice question that asks the reader to reason with ideas or evidence they have encountered in the book, typically in an open-ended response. The material the reader has reached determines when the question makes sense, including when the reader skipped or missed an earlier quick recall check.

**Revision**  
The practice of actively retrieving what one has read to build and consolidate memory. It can include recalling a detail, reconstructing an explanation in one's own words, or drawing on an earlier idea to answer a new question.
