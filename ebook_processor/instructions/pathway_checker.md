
## The graduated pathway

In this book a question has one of two roles, and its role is given with it.

### Ephemeral questions

An ephemeral question is asked **inside** the section, right after the paragraph named with it, with the text hidden. The reader recalls the answer in a few words and reads on. Nothing is graded and the question is not kept for later practice. Judge it by the same criteria, read like this:

- `supported_by_section` and `answerable_from_section`: by the paragraphs of this section **up to and including the paragraph it follows**. The reader has not read further. Cite only those paragraphs.
- `accepts_paraphrase`: a few words or one short sentence is a complete answer here. False only when the answer is vague or does not answer the question.
- `stands_alone`: a reader who has just read up to that paragraph can understand it with the text hidden. It may lean on what was just read. False only for a bare pronoun or pointer that such a reader could not resolve.
- `does_not_give_away_answer`: as for any question. When the question lists a practice question it supports, it is also false when the ephemeral question or its answer states or plainly hints at the answer or the conclusion of that practice question: it rehearses the material and leaves the conclusion to the reader.

A check placed in the middle of an argument, or one that asks for something of no use for following the text, gets a `minor_note`. It does not fail for that.

### Practice questions

A practice question is asked at the end of its section and later in spaced practice, as described above. Some are marked **higher-order**, with the kind of reasoning they ask for. For a higher-order question:

- It may draw on **earlier paragraphs** of the book. They are listed with the question, and their text is given under EARLIER PARAGRAPHS. The reader has read them. Read `supported_by_section` as: every fact the answer uses is stated in this section or in those earlier paragraphs. Read `answerable_from_section` as: a reader who has read the book up to the end of this section could answer from this section and those earlier paragraphs. You may cite the earlier paragraphs listed, beside paragraphs of the section. Text that is neither in the section nor listed does not count, even if it is true.
- The answer may contain a step the text does not state in so many words: an explanation that connects stated facts, a comparison, an interpretation. That step does not fail `supported_by_section` when every fact it rests on is stated in the cited paragraphs and the step follows from them. An answer that brings in a fact from nowhere still fails. For narrative, an answer that reveals later events still fails.
- When the question lists EVIDENCE A GOOD ANSWER ACCOUNTS FOR, several answers are defensible and the reference answer is one of them. `supported_by_section` is false when a statement of that evidence is not stated in the cited paragraphs. `accepts_paraphrase` is true when the evidence would let a reader judge fairly an answer that differs from the reference answer. It is false when the evidence is so thin, or so tied to the reference answer's own conclusion, that only that conclusion could be marked Correct.
- A RECALL CUE listed with a question is shown to a reader who asks for help before answering. `supported_by_section` covers the cue's answer too. `does_not_give_away_answer` is false when the cue states or plainly hints at the practice question's answer.
- A question marked higher-order that only asks for something the cited text states outright in one place is a recall question. That is not a failure: say so in `minor_note`.
